# CLAUDE.md — Project Context & Engineering Guidelines for `hull-deck-insulation`

## 1. Project Overview
`hull-deck-insulation` documents the post-fire structural fiberglass remediation and **3-zone thermal/acoustic insulation refit** on the **2009 Beneteau Oceanis 40 (*Novella*)**, focused on the starboard aft cabin hull topsides, bulkheads, and overhead deck/headliner cavities.

Primary reference document: [`hull-deck-insulation-guide.md`](hull-deck-insulation-guide.md).

---

## 2. Core Engineering Rules & Sequencing Constraints (Do Not Violate)

1. **Mandatory Structural Prep Before Insulation:**
   * All charred gelcoat and heat-affected fiberglass must be ground down to fresh, solid laminate first.
   * All structural bulkhead and stringer tabbing must use **`1708` biaxial fiberglass cloth and `100%` solids structural marine epoxy resin** (never polyester resin on aged secondary bonds), and must be 100% cured, amine-blush washed, and inspected *before* applying any cork or foam.
2. **Zone-Specific Material Assignment:**
   * **Curved Hull Sides / Topsides $\rightarrow$ Water-Based Spray Cork (`3.5–5.5 mm` in `2–3` coats):** Never specify kerfed rigid foam boards or contact-cemented EVA rolls on curved hull skins (they trap air pockets that condense moisture behind the insulation). Spray cork bonds monolithically and flexes elastically with the hull under sail.
   * **Overhead Deck / Headliner Dead-Air Cavities $\rightarrow$ `2-lb` Low-Expansion Closed-Cell Polyurethane Pour Foam (`~R-6/in`):** Always specify drilled fill **and** adjacent open vent relief holes, and inject in **small, calculated incremental lifts** (`60–120 mL` liquid per shot). Never over-pack or pour large single shots, which generate hydraulic expansion pressure that distorts ("pillows") or debonds the molded fiberglass ceiling liner. Always verify overhead wiring is in conduit before foaming.
   * **Flat Bulkheads & Cabinetry Backs $\rightarrow$ Closed-Cell Extruded Polystyrene (`XPS`) Rigid Boards (`~R-5/in`):** Restrict `XPS` strictly to flat vertical surfaces where full-contact bedding eliminates air voids.
   * **Never Specify `EVA` Foam for Hull/Cabin Lining:** Explicitly rejected due to adhesive failure in humid marine environments, lower thermal resistance per inch, and poor fire/off-gassing safety.
